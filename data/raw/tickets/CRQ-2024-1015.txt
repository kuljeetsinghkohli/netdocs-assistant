DESCRIPTION:
Following the BGP route leak incident in 2024 (INC-2024-0930), a full audit of all BGP route-maps and prefix-lists on LON-DC01-RTR01 and NYC-DC01-RTR01 was scheduled. The audit identified:
1. Stale prefix-list PL-OLD-EMEA (references decommissioned subnets 10.9.0.0/20 — no longer assigned).
2. Route-map RM-FROM-BT-IN contained a permit-all fallthrough clause that was overly permissive.
3. A `permit 20` clause in RM-TO-BT-OUT had no match condition (implicit permit any — this was the root cause of the 2024-0930 route leak).

This change removes stale config and tightens the outbound policy.

IMPLEMENTATION PLAN:
LON-DC01-RTR01:
1. Remove stale prefix-list:
   no ip prefix-list PL-OLD-EMEA
2. Tighten RM-FROM-BT-IN — remove the final overly permissive permit clause:
   route-map RM-FROM-BT-IN
    no permit 9000
   Add explicit deny:
   route-map RM-FROM-BT-IN deny 9999
3. Remove the no-match permit clause from RM-TO-BT-OUT:
   route-map RM-TO-BT-OUT
    no permit 20
4. Soft-reset outbound: clear ip bgp 172.16.0.1 soft out
5. Verify only expected prefixes are advertised: show ip bgp neighbors 172.16.0.1 advertised-routes | count
   (expected: 1 — the 10.0.0.0/8 summary)

Repeat equivalent steps on NYC-DC01-RTR01.

ROLLBACK PLAN:
If any BGP session is disrupted:
1. Re-add the removed route-map clauses.
2. Soft reset outbound.
3. Verify session stability.

TEST PLAN:
- Confirm advertised-routes count = 1 (10.0.0.0/8 only) on both routers.
- No BGP session resets during or after change.
- Monitor for 15 minutes post-change.

OUTCOME: Successful. Stale prefix-lists removed. Route-map tightened. Advertised route count confirmed as 1. No session disruptions.
